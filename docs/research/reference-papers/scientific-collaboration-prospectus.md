# Scientific collaboration prospectus

**First invitation — help interpret one disagreement between published drought methods**

`climate_indices` is an open-source Python library for calculating environmental indicators from climate time series. The code is a fork of work originally developed by NIDIS/NCEI/NOAA for drought monitoring. James Adams maintains the library independently today. It implements standardized precipitation and water-balance indices, potential evapotranspiration, the Palmer family, and methods for several other hazards. Project #40 focuses on drought and evapotranspiration. It brings together the methods, source records, and comparison plans needed to test specific claims about those calculations. [Library and release](https://github.com/monocongo/climate_indices/releases/tag/v3.2.0) · [Historical NOAA record](https://www.drought.gov/data-maps-tools/source-code-climate-and-drought-indices-python-spi-spei-pet) · [Project #40](https://github.com/users/monocongo/projects/40)

EarthFlux provides a way to inspect results generated with the library. Its public Scenario Lab shows precomputed drought series for the Apalachicola–Chattahoochee–Flint basin, where readers can compare selected recipes and see which inputs and method choices a result uses. That preview illustrates how a research finding could be presented with its assumptions attached. The first review below concerns an upstream algorithm and can be done independently of the site. [Scenario Lab](https://earthflux.org/lab/) · [Place method](https://earthflux.org/place/method/) · [Known limits](https://earthflux.org/place/known-limits/)

## What the evidence covers

The library publishes validation checks by method. Standard Palmer outputs have a retrospective comparison against NOAA climate-division data, with documented differences in forcing and precision. Log-logistic Standardized Precipitation Evapotranspiration Index (SPEI) has an input-matched comparison with SPEIbase in three U.S. climate divisions. The reported agreement is qualified by a shared fitting routine. The NOAA Evaporative Demand Drought Index (EDDI) check uses paired inputs and outputs at one location. Each check has a specific inference. Agreement on a fixture, fidelity to a historical program and suitability for an applied analysis need distinct evidence. [Validation record](https://github.com/monocongo/climate_indices/blob/380aa01f/VALIDATION.md)

Project #40 has published an equation-to-code record for Palmer and a numerical study of two spell-tracking recursions using the same supplied moisture anomalies and duration factors. Its report records when pre-specified expectations failed and how the attribution procedure changed after exploratory work. The project has also documented where comparison tools share code or numerical routines, and where relationships remain uncertain. The projected cross-package study and independent scientific review remain open. [Palmer provenance](https://github.com/monocongo/climate_indices/pull/1659) · [Recursion study](https://github.com/monocongo/climate_indices/pull/1658) · [Lineage map](https://github.com/monocongo/climate_indices/pull/1667)

## A first review in 60–90 minutes

We would like a Palmer or hydroclimate scientist to examine one reduced example where the two rules for establishing wet or dry spells give different results. The case uses five active months embedded in a 12-month sequence. Its input anomaly series and duration factors are held fixed, so the reader can inspect the recursion without reconstructing a soil water balance or fitting parameters. The documentation links the conventional Palmer approach to a threshold magnitude of 1 and the Wells method to 0.5. The exact equality convention deserves particular care. [Review question](https://github.com/monocongo/climate_indices/issues/1631) · [Pinned sequences and outputs](https://github.com/monocongo/climate_indices/blob/380aa01f/docs/research/reference-papers/palmer-recursion-divergence-sequences.json) · [Wells et al.](https://journals.ametsoc.org/view/journals/clim/17/12/1520-0442_2004_017_2335_aspdsi_2.0.co_2.xml)

We would ask you to mark which parts of a proposed explanation follow from the publications and the example, identify where the evidence is ambiguous, and suggest one test or source that would change your assessment. A qualified or negative conclusion would be useful. The example can clarify a mechanism under specified conditions. A broader assessment of monitoring skill, portability to different climates, or how often the mechanism matters in practice would require separate evidence.

The maintainer would prepare a short note, cited source passages, the inputs and both outputs, and optional rerun instructions. A written response by email is enough. You can also choose to rerun the case or discuss it in a brief call. Engineering support covers data preparation, code, and follow-up computations. The scientific reviewer shapes the interpretation and can propose a different question or decline to endorse the wording.

## Further questions and credit

Other bounded collaborations could examine whether a dry-station Standardized Precipitation Index (SPI) comparison isolates distribution, calibration and zero handling, or whether a monthly FAO-56 Penman–Monteith example makes a sound conformance check. A constructive result could be a reusable reference case, a methods note, or the design of a larger joint comparison. [SPI comparison protocol](https://github.com/monocongo/climate_indices/issues/1604) · [Monthly evapotranspiration question](https://github.com/monocongo/climate_indices/issues/1623)

We would return a short record of the case, the feedback and any resulting change or disagreement for your review. We would agree on credit and permissions before sharing it. The record may support a later funding request for scientific time; you can decline that use. Contributions to a future publication would follow the intellectual work and responsibilities involved. A review concerns the specific method and claim in the record.

If this question overlaps with your work, reply to the person who shared this prospectus or contact the project maintainer through the public repository. [Project repository](https://github.com/monocongo/climate_indices)
